Enter your dog's age to see a estimate of the human age that matches it, based on a 2020 DNA study. It takes a few seconds and stores nothing.

How does the estimate work?

The estimate uses a formula from a 2020 study in the journal Cell Systems, which tracked chemical changes in DNA as 104 dogs, mostly Labrador retrievers, aged and compared them with human patterns: human age = 16 × ln(dog age) + 31, with dog age in years.1 It is more current than the old "multiply by 7" rule, because dogs age fastest early in life and more slowly later.1

Why does Google say 28 for a 3-year-old?

Size-based charts, like the one from Zoetis Petcare, give lower numbers (28 for a 3-year-old under 50 lb) because they work differently and are only rough rules of thumb.2 This calculator uses DNA changes instead, so it gives a higher number. Neither says anything about your dog's health.

How accurate is it for my breed?

It was built mostly on one breed, Labrador retrievers, so treat the result as a fun, rough guide for any dog. The authors note other breeds could give different results.1 Dogs of different sizes and breeds have different lifespans, so the match will not be exact for every dog. It is not medical advice, and it says nothing about your dog's health.

What should I use this for?

Use it as a conversation starter, or to picture where your dog is in life. For anything about your dog's care at a given age, ask your vet.
