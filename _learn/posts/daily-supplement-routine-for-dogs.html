Why does a daily routine matter for dogs?

A daily routine matters because pets are creatures of habit and enjoy their routines.1 Each pet and owner develops their own rhythm of morning walks, afternoon playtime and consistent feeding times,1 and a daily chew slots easily into a rhythm like that.

Young dogs benefit from consistency. Cornell's Riney Canine Health Center notes that routines give them structure and predictability. Keeping a routine helps puppies adapt to living with us, and consistent interactions help them always know what to expect.2

Regular activity belongs in the routine too. The Merck Veterinary Manual notes that dogs need regular exercise to stay healthy, and dogs that get enough exercise are generally healthier and happier.3 A walk at the same time each day is easy to keep on the calendar.

A routine helps you as well. Owners who form deep connections with their pets learn what is normal for them, and through daily routines, body language and eating habits, owners can quickly detect when something is off.1 If you notice a change in your dog's appetite, energy or behavior, tell your vet.

When is the best time to give dog supplements?

The best time is the time the label gives, tied to a moment that already happens every day. If the label names a time of day or a meal, start there.

Mealtime is an easy anchor because it is already on a schedule. A typical feeding schedule is 1 or 2 daily meals for adult dogs, and 2 to 4 for puppies depending on age.4 Whatever your dog's meal pattern, pick the meal you never skip and make the chew part of it.

If your mornings are rushed, the walk or the evening meal can work just as well. What matters is that the cue is the same every day. Grooming can sit on the same calendar, and our guide on how often to bathe a dog covers baths and brushing.

Should I give my dog's supplements with food?

Give your dog's supplement with food when the label says to, since the label sets how and when to give that product. If it says to give the chew with a meal, do that.

If the label does not say, any steady daily moment works, and your vet can help you choose. Tying the chew to a meal is simply an easy way to remember it, since the meal is happening anyway. How do I remember to give my dog's supplement every day?

Make the chew part of a habit you already have, so the old habit can remind you of the new one. A few small setups help:

  • Give the chew at the same moment each day, right after the same cue.
  • Keep the jar in the same spot, close to where the cue happens.
  • Put 1 person in charge, or use a shared checklist so no one gives a second chew by mistake.
  • Mark each day on a calendar, a note on the fridge or a phone reminder.

Where you keep the jar also matters. The FDA advises keeping pet treats in a secure location so a pet cannot eat a whole supply at once, and storing food where a persistent pet cannot get to it.5 Treat a jar of chews the same way: easy for you to see, hard for your dog to reach.

Keep pet food in its original container so the product name, lot number and "best by" date stay handy, and store dry food in a cool, dry place below 80°F (about 27°C).5 For a supplement, keep it in its own jar and follow any storage directions on the label.

What if I miss a day of my dog's supplement?

If you miss a day, give the next chew at your usual time and carry on. Check the label, or ask your vet, before giving anything extra to make up for it.

A missed day can be a sign the cue needs adjusting, not a reason to give up. The goal is a daily routine you can keep, so if mornings keep slipping, move the chew to the walk or the evening meal.

How do I keep track of my dog's supplements for my vet?

Keep a simple list of every supplement your dog takes, with how much and how often. The American Veterinary Medical Association suggests being ready to list any medications your pet is receiving, including supplements, with the amount, frequency and duration, and even bringing them with their instructions to the visit.6

A note on your phone does the job. Add the product name, the amount on the label, the time of day you give it and the date you started. If the label lists omega-3s, our guide to what EPA and DHA are explains each name.

Frequently asked questions.

Can I give my dog's supplement at breakfast?

Yes, if the label allows it. Breakfast is an easy anchor because it already happens every day.

Is morning or evening better for my dog's supplement?

Follow the label first. If it does not name a time, pick the moment you are least likely to miss, and ask your vet if you are unsure.

Where should I keep my dog's chews?

Keep them somewhere you will see every day but your dog cannot reach. The FDA advises keeping pet treats in a secure location so a pet cannot eat a whole supply at once.5

What should I tell my vet about my dog's supplements?

Tell your vet the name of each product, how much you give, how often and for how long. It helps to be ready to list any medications your dog receives, including supplements.6
