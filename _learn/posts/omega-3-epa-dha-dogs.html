Omega-3s are a family of polyunsaturated fatty acids, and 3 members named in dog nutrition are EPA (eicosapentaenoic acid), DHA (docosahexaenoic acid) and ALA (alpha-linolenic acid).1 EPA and DHA are essential fatty acids for dogs, so they should be part of a dog's diet.1

What makes a fatty acid essential for dogs?

For dogs, essential fatty acids include the omega-3s EPA, DHA and ALA, and they belong in a dog's diet.1

Dogs can turn ALA into EPA and DHA, but only at a quite limited rate, so EPA and DHA should come from the diet itself.1

Where do EPA and DHA come from?

EPA and DHA can come from fish oil, an efficient source of both.1 In one study, the dog food contained fish oil as a source of EPA and DHA, and EPA made up 4.31% and DHA 3.01% of the food's fatty acids.1 Commercial dog food also usually contains some ALA.1

Do puppies need DHA?

Yes, puppies need DHA, an omega-3 fatty acid, to support brain development, along with more protein for growing muscles and calcium and phosphorus for growing bones.2 High-quality puppy food supplies these nutrients, and a vet can advise on the right diet for a growing dog.2

How are omega-3s different from omega-6s?

Omega-3s and omega-6s are 2 separate families of polyunsaturated fatty acids, and dogs need some of each in their diet.1 Linoleic acid is an omega-6 that commercial dog food usually contains.1

Arachidonic acid is another omega-6, and a dog's body makes it from linoleic acid.1 In the same way, ALA is the starting point for EPA and DHA.1 Both conversions use the same enzymes, so turning ALA into EPA competes with turning linoleic acid into arachidonic acid.1

How do omega-3s show up on a label?

Omega-3 sources show up on a label by name in the ingredient list.3 Under rules from the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA), ingredients must be listed by their common or usual name, in descending order of predominance by weight.3 That order counts water, so the most prominent ingredient by weight, including water, is listed first.4

To sort what you find, work through these 3 steps:

  1. Find the ingredient list, since federal labeling rules require a listing of all ingredients.3
  2. Look for fish oil, a source of EPA and DHA.1
  3. Note where fish oil sits in the list, since ingredients run from most to least by weight, water included.3,4
