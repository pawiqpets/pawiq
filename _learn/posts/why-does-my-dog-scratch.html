Fleas are the most common cause of allergic skin disease worldwide, and airborne and food allergies can make a dog itchy too.1,2 Scratching is too much when it disrupts your dog's normal behavior or sleep, or your sleep.1

Contact your vet if the scratching comes with any of these signs.

  • Hair loss, red skin, scratch marks or a rash, which mean the itching is more than normal.1
  • Wounds, scabs or scaling where your dog scratches.2
  • Signs of a skin or ear infection, which scratching can lead to.2
  • Scratching that disrupts your dog's sleep, or yours.1

Itching shows up as more than scratching

An itchy dog does not always scratch, since biting, licking, rubbing, rolling, head shaking and scooting can also mean a dog feels itchy.1 A veterinarian can help judge whether your dog's itching is normal or too much, and the Texas A&M small animal hospital rates itching on a scale of 1 to 10.1

Fleas can cause itching even when you see none

Fleas will not always be seen, and flea control still matters even when no fleas are found.1,3 Fleas are the most common external parasite in dogs, and they cause intense itching and often hair loss or skin infections.3 Even a dog with no flea allergy will scratch at the annoyance of flea bites.4

Extremely sensitive dogs are likely to be virtually free of fleas because they groom themselves so much.4 In a sensitive dog, even a small amount of flea saliva can cause a massive allergic response.1

Most of the flea population lives in the environment, so successful flea control also covers the home and yard.3 Wash all bedding and vacuum often.3 With a massive infestation or a severe flea allergy, treating the home may still be needed: wash pet blankets and throw rugs, and vacuum where pets sleep, including crevices in sofas and chairs.4 Keep every pet in the household on year-round, consistent flea prevention.3

Airborne and food allergies can make a dog itchy

Skin allergies are one of the most common causes of skin problems in dogs.5 Airborne and food allergies can both cause itchy skin.2 Dog skin allergies often show up as itchy skin, repeated licking at the paws or feet, or ear and skin infections that keep coming back.5 Allergy signs from things in the air usually start between 6 months and 3 years of age, and they are usually seasonal but may be seen all year.2

Food allergies are less common than airborne allergies in pets.2 With a food allergy, the itching changes little from one season to the next.2 The foods dogs are most often allergic to include beef, chicken, eggs, corn, wheat, soy and milk.2

Infections and parasites can cause the same signs as allergies, so those are checked first.1 A food allergy is diagnosed by feeding a limited-ingredient diet and seeing if the itching resolves, with a test plan from your veterinarian.2,5 During the trial, only new food ingredients can be fed, and that includes treats and table food.2

Flea control and diet trials take weeks to months

Flea control and a food elimination trial each take weeks to months, as the timelines below show.

Keep an itch log for your vet

A simple itch log can help you describe the itching to your vet. Each evening, rate the itch from 1 to 10, note whether it was worse at night or woke anyone up, and write down any change in food, treats or flea control.

Where your dog scratches is a clue worth writing down too. Flea allergy classically shows up as crusted, itchy skin over the hips, the base of the tail and the thighs.4 Airborne allergies most often affect the feet, face, ears, front legs and belly.2

If you bathe your dog during this time, add the date to the log. For timing by coat type, see how often to bathe a dog, and browse more Learn guides for everyday care.

Talk to your vet. Bring your itch log and a list of everything your dog eats, including treats, to the visit. Skin disease from airborne allergies, called atopic dermatitis, is lifelong and needs long-term management and regular veterinary exams.2

Frequently asked questions.

Why does my dog scratch their ears so much?

A dog with airborne allergies often scratches at the ears, since the ears are among the areas most often affected.2 Scratching from allergies can also lead to ear infections, and head shaking can be another sign that a dog feels itchy.1,2

Can laundry detergent make my dog itchy?

Allergies to cleaners or laundry detergents are almost never seen in dogs and cats.1
