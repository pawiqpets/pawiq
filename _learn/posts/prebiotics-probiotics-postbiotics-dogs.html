Probiotics are live microorganisms, prebiotics are substances that a dog's own resident microbes selectively use, and postbiotics are microorganisms that have been deliberately inactivated, or their components. Each has its own scientific consensus definition.1,2,3

What is a probiotic?

A probiotic is made of live microorganisms. The standard definition is "live microorganisms which when administered in adequate amounts confer a health benefit on the host," from the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations and the World Health Organization. An expert panel from the International Scientific Association for Probiotics and Prebiotics (ISAPP) reaffirmed it in a 2014 consensus statement.1

The key word is live. That is what separates a probiotic from the other 2 terms.

What is a prebiotic?

A prebiotic is a substance that a dog's own resident microbes selectively use. A 2017 expert consensus defines it as "a substrate that is selectively utilized by host microorganisms conferring a health benefit."2

The definition also applies to prebiotics for animals.2

What is a postbiotic?

A postbiotic is a preparation of microorganisms that have been deliberately inactivated, or their components. That definition, published in 2021 by an ISAPP expert panel, is the newest of the 3: a "preparation of inanimate microorganisms and/or their components that confers a health benefit on the host."3

It does not have to come from a probiotic, and the host can include companion animals.3 Researchers have studied inactivated forms of both bacteria and yeasts, such as the yeast Saccharomyces boulardii.3

Frequently asked questions.

Are prebiotics and probiotics the same thing?

No. Probiotics are live microorganisms. Prebiotics are substances that a dog's own resident microbes selectively use.1,2

Is a postbiotic just a dead probiotic?

Not exactly. Under the 2021 definition, a postbiotic is a preparation of deliberately inactivated microorganisms or their components. It does not have to come from a probiotic.3

Do the definitions apply to dogs?

Each definition is written around a host rather than people specifically. The prebiotic definition applies to animals, and under the postbiotic definition the host can include companion animals.2,3

What is a synbiotic?

A synbiotic combines live microorganisms with a substance the host's own microbes selectively use. The 2021 postbiotic consensus statement gives the current definition: "a mixture comprising live microorganisms and substrate(s) selectively utilized by host microorganisms that confers a health benefit on the host."3

Which of the 3 was defined most recently?

Postbiotics. The current definition was published in 2021, after the 2014 probiotic and 2017 prebiotic consensus statements.1,2,3
