There is no single temperature that is too cold for every dog, but veterinary schools give clear markers. Washington State University's veterinary hospital says to bring pets indoors or provide adequate shelter when temperatures dip below freezing (32°F, which is 0°C), and Purdue veterinarians say puppies and short-haired pets should come inside below 40°F (about 4°C).1,2,5

Coat and age affect how well a dog handles the cold, and small dogs may need extra protection. The American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) advises knowing your pet's tolerance for the cold and adjusting accordingly.3,4

Is there a single temperature that is too cold for dogs?

No, there is no single number that fits every dog. Dr. Gene Pavlovsky, director of the University of Illinois Veterinary Medicine South Clinic, says: "There is no straightforward answer to how cold is too cold and how long is too long to remain outside."5 For most dogs it is probably safe to be outside for a very short period, just long enough for a bathroom break, even in extreme cold.5

The AVMA says to know your own pet's tolerance for the cold and adjust from there.4

What temperatures do veterinary schools mention?

Puppies and short-haired pets should come inside below 40°F (about 4°C), and outdoor pets below 20°F (about -7°C). Below freezing, 32°F (0°C), bring pets indoors or provide adequate shelter.1,2

The AVMA says dogs and cats generally should be kept inside in cold weather, and no pet should be left outside for long periods in below-freezing weather.4 If you are cold, your pets are most likely cold too.2

If your dog must stay outdoors, make sure it has a warm, solid shelter against the wind and unfrozen drinking water; a good shelter has 3 enclosed sides, is raised off the ground and holds plenty of bedding such as straw or hay.2,4

Which dogs feel the cold more?

Puppies, older dogs and short-haired dogs are the most vulnerable to hypothermia, and small dogs may need extra protection.3

  • Coat: short-haired dogs are particularly vulnerable to the cold.1
  • Size: small and short-haired dogs may need coats even for short walks in cold, windy weather.3
  • Age: puppies should come inside below 40°F (about 4°C), and very young and older animals are among the most vulnerable to hypothermia.2,3

How can you tell your dog is too cold?

A dog that is too cold may shiver, slow down or lift its paws off the ground. A dog that is picking up its feet or seems reluctant to walk may be too cold, and shivering, anxiety and moving slowly are signs of hypothermia.3

The physical signs of hypothermia include shivering, irregular heart rate and breathing, and visible exhaustion.1 Frostbite often affects a dog's paw pads, nose and ears, and can make a dog appear to prance when walking.1

If you suspect hypothermia or notice any concerning signs of cold-related injuries, seek veterinary care immediately; you can also cover your dog with blankets and let your dog warm up gradually.1,4 If your dog is whining, shivering, seems anxious or weak, or slows down or stops moving, get your dog back inside quickly, and consult your veterinarian right away if you suspect hypothermia or frostbite.4

Talk to your vet. Ask your veterinarian how much cold your dog can handle, especially if your dog is a puppy, a senior or has a health condition. If your dog shows signs of being too cold, call your vet.

How long should winter walks be?

Winter walks should be shorter than usual when it is very cold. Keep walks short and watch for signs of hypothermia.3,4 If the clock change has moved your walks into darker evenings, our guide on does daylight saving time affect dogs can help you plan.

Some dogs may need a coat. Consider a sweater or dog coat if your dog has a short coat or seems bothered by the cold.4

How do you protect your dog's paws in winter?

To protect your dog's paws in winter, consider booties, check the paws often and clean them after walks. Booties can protect paw pads from the cold and from rock salt; just make sure they fit.1,4

  • Before walks: consider keeping the hair between the paw pads short to help prevent ice balls, and applying paw balm to the pads before each walk.3
  • During the walk: longer-haired dogs may collect ice and snow on their feet, so check them regularly.3
  • After the walk: wipe or wash your dog's feet, legs and belly to remove de-icing products, antifreeze and other chemicals.4

Check your dog's paws often for signs of irritation or injury, too.4 For full baths between walks, our guide on how often to bathe a dog covers timing by coat type.

What winter hazards should dog owners know about?

Antifreeze, de-icers and frozen water are 3 hazards veterinary sources flag.3,4 The active ingredient in most brands of antifreeze is ethylene glycol, which is sweet-tasting and highly toxic, so store it out of reach and clean up any spills right away.3

Salt and most de-icers can also be toxic to dogs, and consider pet-safe de-icers on your property.3,4 Stay away from frozen ponds, lakes and streams as well.4

Frequently asked questions.

What temperature is too cold for a dog to be outside?

There is no single number for every dog. Washington State says to bring pets indoors or provide adequate shelter below freezing (32°F, which is 0°C). Purdue veterinarians say puppies and short-haired pets should come inside below 40°F (about 4°C) and outdoor pets below 20°F (about -7°C).1,2

How long can a dog stay out in freezing weather?

Washington State says most dogs, especially those with thinner coats, should not be exposed to freezing temperatures for more than 10 to 15 minutes without access to a warmer space.1

Do dogs need coats in winter?

Some do. Consider a sweater or dog coat if your dog has a short coat or seems bothered by the cold, and keep in mind that small and short-haired dogs may need one even for short walks in cold, windy weather.3,4

What are the signs a dog is too cold?

Signs a dog may be too cold include whining, shivering, anxiety, moving slowly, lifting the paws or seeming reluctant to walk. If your dog is whining, shivering, seems anxious or weak, or slows down or stops moving, get your dog back inside quickly and consult your veterinarian if you suspect hypothermia or frostbite.3,4

How cold is too cold to walk a dog?

There is no set cutoff. Shorten walks in very cold weather and watch for signs of hypothermia.3,4,5
