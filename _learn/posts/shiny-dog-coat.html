To help keep a dog's coat healthy, feed a well-balanced diet that includes omega fatty acids, brush regularly, and bathe in a way that doesn't strip the coat's protective oils.1,2 A well-balanced, high-quality diet, including one with omega fatty acids and biotin (a B vitamin), will benefit a dog's skin and coat.1

What complete and balanced means for dog food

A complete and balanced diet is one shown to meet the nutrient profiles or feeding protocols of the Association of American Feed Control Officials (AAFCO), the only methods it recognizes for that claim.3 There are 2 dog food profiles, one for growth and reproduction and one for adult maintenance, and each lists minimum levels for nutrients.3

Minimum protein, fat and linoleic acid in dog food

A food that meets an AAFCO dog food profile has to reach minimum levels for protein, fat and fatty acids, including the omega-6 linoleic acid.3 The levels are on a dry matter basis, which means the food's water is left out of the math, while the guaranteed analysis on a label is usually as fed, so label numbers need converting to dry matter before you compare them.3

When dry skin or heavy shedding is a reason to see your vet

Dry skin and coat or heavy shedding can have a medical cause, and these problems typically won't resolve without intervention, so see your vet if you suspect one.1 Possible causes include flea or tick reactions, allergies to things like pollen or grass, food allergies, an unbalanced diet, and fungal, bacterial or yeast infections.1

  • If your dog has inflamed skin, open sores or seems uncomfortable when touched, skip the bath and call your vet.4

Brush at least 3 to 4 times a week for 5 to 10 minutes

Brushing a dog's coat at least 3 to 4 times a week for 5 to 10 minutes can help soften the coat and reduce shedding, according to the Texas A&M College of Veterinary Medicine & Biomedical Sciences.1 Its suggestions are a brush or comb with metal or plastic-tipped wire pins for short coats, a deshedding brush for thick coats, and a slicker brush for curly coats.1 Brush before a bath as well, to clear loose hair and mats that trap water and shampoo against the skin.4

Baths help the coat when they don't strip its oils

Bathing a dog too often can strip the coat of its protective oils, although a weekly bath with a veterinary shampoo is generally safe.2 Many healthy dogs do well with a bath every 4 to 12 weeks, though coat type and lifestyle change that.4 Dish soap and human shampoo strip natural oils from fur, so use a shampoo made for dogs.2

Use lukewarm water, rinse until you feel no slick spots and see no foam, press towels against the coat instead of rubbing, and dry the dog completely.4 For timing by coat type, see how often to bathe a dog, step by step.

Talk to your vet. Bring up any change in your dog's coat, and mention what your dog eats.

Frequently asked questions.

Should you shave a double-coated dog?

A double-coated dog usually does not need a full shave unless the coat is matted, and frequent brushing is recommended instead to remove excess hair from thick fur.5 In double-coated breeds such as Siberian Huskies and Labradors, the undercoat may grow back first and interfere with the growth of the guard hairs, which can leave an altered coat.5

Does a dog's hair turn gray with age?

Yes, a graying coat is one of the easy-to-spot outward signs of aging in pets, along with a slower pace.6
