Is Christmas tree water bad for dogs?

It can be. Tree water can upset a dog's stomach, and needles, tinsel, lights and holiday plants carry their own risks. Here is how to set up a safer season.

Written by the pawIQ team · Last reviewed November 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Yes, Christmas tree water can be bad for dogs. Drinking it can cause stomach and gut signs, and any product added to keep the tree fresh should be checked to make sure it is safe for pets.1,2

Needles, tinsel, lights, holiday plants and candles all deserve a second look before December 25.

What happens if a dog drinks Christmas tree water?

A dog that drinks Christmas tree water can get stomach and gut signs, according to the University of Pennsylvania School of Veterinary Medicine (Penn Vet).1 Call your vet right away if your dog is not acting right, and have the label of any tree water additive handy.2,3 Treating a poisoning tends to work best when it starts soon after the pet eats it.2

Tree water additives are a second concern. If you use a product meant to keep the tree fresh, read the label to confirm it is safe for pets.2 Even with their own water bowl, pets can find a new source of water, like the tree stand, enticing.2

Are Christmas trees toxic to dogs?

Christmas trees are not generally harmful to dogs, but pine needles can cause eye trauma.3 If chewing on greenery is a habit, our guide on why do dogs eat grass covers that behavior.

How to keep your dog away from the Christmas tree

Keep your dog away from the Christmas tree by securing the tree and gating off the room when no one is watching.3,4 A tree that is not secured can tip over,1 so anchor it so it can take bumps and your dog's zoomies.4 Baby gates are an easy way to block off the tree room.3

Christmas decorations that can make dogs sick

Many Christmas decorations can make dogs sick if they chew or swallow them, especially tinsel, ribbon and ornaments.2,3 Eating tinsel, ribbon or other string-like items can cause serious damage to the intestine.2 Signs can include vomiting, diarrhea, belly pain and fever.2 Swallowed tinsel can become stuck in the gut and may need surgery to remove.1

Ornaments carry a different set of risks. Glass ornaments and hooks break easily, and a pet that swallows the splinters can cut its mouth, food pipe or intestines.1 Dough ornaments are high in salt and can cause vomiting, diarrhea and, in severe cases, seizures, so keep your dog away from them.1

When no one is watching, keep your dog out of rooms with decorations so it cannot chew or swallow ornaments, lights, wires or ribbon.3

Christmas lights, cords and electrical shock

Christmas lights and cords can be dangerous if your dog chews them. Lights are another thing pets like to chew, and chewing on cords can cause an electrical shock.2 A shock can cause burns, trouble breathing, an abnormal heart rhythm and even death.2 Puppies seem to find wires especially tempting.1

A few habits lower the risk:

  • Check cords for bite marks, loose or frayed wires and signs of a short circuit.2
  • Use grounded three-prong extension cords and follow the maker's directions.2
  • Block off decorated rooms when your dog is alone.3

Call a veterinarian immediately if your dog has been hurt by an electrical shock.2

Mistletoe, holly and other holiday plants to keep from dogs

Mistletoe and holly are the holiday plants to keep away from your dog, while poinsettias are relatively safe.3 The risk differs from plant to plant.1

Poinsettias get a scary reputation, but their toxicity is often exaggerated.1,2 Their sap can still irritate the mouth, and a dog that eats part of the plant may vomit or lose its appetite.1,2 Signs should only last an hour or 2, and if they persist, call your vet.1 Holly's spiny leaves can hurt the mouth, tongue and lips, and eating the leaves or berries can cause vomiting, diarrhea and drooling.1

Mistletoe can cause anything from an upset stomach to serious illness, so call your vet right away if your dog may have eaten any.2,4 Signs can include vomiting, diarrhea and trouble breathing.2

Lilies are mainly a danger to cats, but lily-of-the-valley and gloriosa lilies are very dangerous to dogs too.5 If you share your home with a cat, leave true lilies and daylilies out.5

Lit candles and the fire risk to dogs

Lit candles are a real risk, because pets do not understand the danger of fire.4 Never leave your dog alone in a room with lit candles.4

Keeping your dog safe with guests over

Give your dog a quiet place to retreat and let guests know the house rules. Strange people, smells and loud sounds can overwhelm a pet, so make sure your dog has a safe, quiet room to go to.4 A nervous dog may do better in a safe space, a separate room or a crate while visitors are over.3

Make sure family, friends and guests know what your dog can and cannot eat.1

Holiday foods are a topic of their own. For the basics on sharing from the table, see what Thanksgiving foods can dogs eat. For cookies, cake and other December treats, read which Christmas foods dogs can't eat.

What to do if your dog eats a decoration or plant

If you suspect your dog ate something it should not, call your own vet or your local emergency vet right away.3,4

You can also call 1 of 2 animal poison control lines. Both charge, or may charge, a consultation fee. Here is what each service's own website says:6,7

  • ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center, (888) 426-4435: its website says a consultation fee may apply but does not list the amount.6
  • Pet Poison Helpline, (855) 764-7661: its website says a $99 fee applies per incident, and all follow-up consultations are included.7

If your dog got into a packaged product, have the label handy when you call.2 If you are traveling, find out ahead of time where you can get veterinary care over the holidays.3

Frequently asked questions.

How do I stop my dog from drinking the Christmas tree water?

Cover the Christmas tree water or block off the tree stand so your dog cannot reach it.8 Christmas tree water can breed bacteria that may cause nausea, diarrhea or stomach upset in pets.8

Are fake Christmas trees toxic to dogs?

Eating artificial Christmas tree material can irritate a dog's stomach and gut.9 If a dog eats enough artificial tree material, a gut blockage is possible.9

Talk to your vet. Every dog is different. If your dog is a puppy, a determined chewer or has a health condition, ask your veterinarian how to set up your home for the holidays and who to call if your clinic is closed.
