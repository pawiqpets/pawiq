How can you tell if your dog is overweight?

The scale is only part of the story. Here is a simple hands-on check of the ribs, waist, tummy and tail base, built from veterinary guidance, plus when to ask your vet.

Written by the pawIQ team · Last reviewed November 13, 2026 · 5 min read

To tell if your dog is overweight, use your hands and eyes, not just the scale. Ribs that are hard to feel under fat, no waist when you look from above and no tummy tuck from the side are signs of too much body fat.1

Why the scale alone is not enough

The American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) says a healthy weight isn't simply a number on a scale.2 Keeping a dog at a healthy weight lowers the risk of health problems such as arthritis and diabetes, and gives the best chance of a long, healthy life.2

Your vet can teach you to assess your dog's body condition by looking at body shape and feeling certain parts of the body.2

What is a dog body condition score?

A dog body condition score describes a dog's body fat and shape, from very thin through ideal to obese.1 It is judged at 4 spots: the ribs, the waist from above, the tummy from the side and the base of the tail.1 These are the 4 areas the Merck Veterinary Manual lists for judging body condition.1

Being overweight means carrying extra body fat.3 In a 2025 study from the Dog Aging Project, vets had recorded the score on a 5-point or 9-point scale.3 On the 9-point scale, the researchers counted 4 to 5 as ideal, 6 to 7 as overweight and 8 to 9 as obese.3

The 4-spot body condition check

To check if your dog is overweight, work through the 4 spots in order, with your dog standing calmly. Use your eyes and your hands, then compare what you find with the table below.1,2

RibsFeel along the sides of the chestRibs easy to feel under a slight layer of fat; you can't see each ribRibs hard to feel under a thick layer of fat

TummyLook from the sideThe tummy tucks upNo tuck; fat hangs from the belly

Is your dog overweight? If every spot matches the Ideal column, your dog is likely at an ideal weight.1 If any spot falls between the 2 columns or matches the Obese column, ask your vet.

The table shows only the ideal and obese ends of the range. A dog can be overweight without being obese, since obesity is the more severe end.3

On a fluffy or long-coated dog, rely on your hands at the ribs and tail base, the 2 spots the table checks by feel.1

Should you be able to feel your dog's ribs?

Yes, you should be able to feel your dog's ribs easily under a slight layer of fat, without seeing each rib.1 Ribs that are hard to feel under a thick layer of fat are a sign of obesity.1

Why extra weight can be easy to miss

Extra weight can be easy to miss, and owners report it less often than vets do.3

That 2025 study compared owner answers with vet body condition scores for 198 dogs.3 Owners and vets disagreed about whether the dog was overweight in 24% of cases.3 Of the 61 dogs the vet's score put in the overweight range, owners called 30 "not overweight."3

The researchers suggest owners may not have learned what extra fat looks like on dogs, especially across different body shapes.3 Owners in the study answered a simple yes or no question about their dog's weight.3 The authors say owners may answer yes less often than they would spot extra weight on a graded score.3

Talk to your vet. Your vet can check your dog's weight and overall health, and help you decide whether that weight is healthy.2 They can work with you on a weight plan, including which food choices and amounts suit your dog.2 An honest look at your dog's weight isn't a judgment of how well you care for your dog.2

Food-loving dogs are more often reported overweight

In the Dog Aging Project study, dogs that were more motivated by food were more likely to be reported as overweight by their owners.3 Food motivation was highest in the sporting breed group, mostly Labrador Retrievers and Golden Retrievers in the study.3

If your dog lives for mealtime, a regular body condition check is worth keeping up. It gives you something concrete to share with your vet.

Making body condition checks a habit

Tie the check to a date you already remember, like the first day of each month. Write down what you felt at each spot, so you can share your notes with your vet.

If you also weigh your dog, note the weight next to your notes and bring both to your next checkup. At each checkup, ask for a body condition score to go in your dog's record. In the 2025 study, researchers had to screen 324 medical records to find 200 with a recent score.3

Frequently asked questions.

How much over ideal weight is overweight for a dog?

A dog that weighs 10% or more above its ideal body weight is considered overweight, and a dog more than 20% above is considered obese.4 On a 9-point body condition score, where 4 or 5 is ideal, each point above ideal equals 10% of the dog's weight.4

Do dogs gain weight after being spayed or neutered?

Yes, spayed and neutered dogs are more at risk of becoming overweight.5 A neutered male dog needs roughly 25% fewer calories, and obesity can follow if his meals are not adjusted.6

Is it normal for older dogs to gain weight?

Overweight or obesity is one of the most common health problems in aging pets, including dogs.7 Pets can become less active with age, and weight gain in senior dogs increases the risk of health problems.7
