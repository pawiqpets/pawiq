How cold is too cold for dogs?

There is no single number, but vet schools give clear markers. Here is what they say about temperature, which dogs feel the cold most, and how to keep winter walks safe.

Written by the pawIQ team · Last reviewed October 28, 2026 · 6 min read

There is no single temperature that is too cold for every dog, but veterinary schools give clear markers. Washington State University's veterinary hospital says to bring pets indoors or provide adequate shelter when temperatures dip below freezing (32°F or 0°C), and Purdue veterinarians say puppies and short-haired pets should come inside below 40°F (about 4°C).1,2

Coat and age affect how well a dog handles the cold, and small dogs may need extra protection. The American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) advises knowing your pet's tolerance for the cold and adjusting accordingly.3,4

Is there a single temperature that is too cold for dogs?

No, there is no single number that fits every dog. Dr. Gene Pavlovsky, director of the University of Illinois Veterinary Medicine South Clinic, puts it simply: "There is no straightforward answer to how cold is too cold and how long is too long to remain outside."5 Most dogs can probably be outside for a very short period, just long enough for a bathroom break, even in extreme cold.5

That is why the AVMA's advice starts with your dog: "Know your pet's tolerance for the cold; adjust accordingly."4

What temperatures do veterinary schools mention?

Purdue veterinarians say puppies and short-haired pets should come inside below 40°F (about 4°C) and outdoor pets below 20°F (about -7°C), while Washington State says to bring pets indoors or provide adequate shelter below 32°F (0°C).1,2

In extreme cold, even outdoor pets should stay inside.4 Here is a handy rule of thumb: if you are cold, your pets are most likely cold too.2

If your dog must stay outdoors, make sure it has insulated shelter and unfrozen drinking water; a good shelter has 3 enclosed sides, is raised off the ground and holds plenty of bedding such as straw or hay.2,4

Which dogs feel the cold more?

Puppies, older dogs and short-haired dogs are the most vulnerable to hypothermia, and small dogs may need extra protection. Dr. Brian Collins, a section chief at the Cornell University Hospital for Animals, says that when it comes to hypothermia, "very young animals, older animals and short-haired breeds are the most vulnerable."3

  • Coat: short-haired dogs are particularly vulnerable to the cold.1
  • Size: small and short-haired dogs may need coats even for short walks in cold, windy weather.3
  • Age: puppies should come inside below 40°F (about 4°C), and very young and older animals are among the most vulnerable to hypothermia.2,3

How can you tell your dog is too cold?

A dog that is too cold may shiver, slow down or lift its paws off the ground. A dog that is picking up its feet or seems reluctant to walk may be too cold, and shivering, anxiety and moving slowly are signs of hypothermia.3

Washington State lists the physical signs of hypothermia as shivering, irregular heart rate and breathing, and visible exhaustion.1 Frostbite often affects a dog's paw pads, nose and ears, and can make a dog appear to prance when walking.1

If you suspect hypothermia or notice any concerning signs of cold-related injuries, seek veterinary care immediately; you can also cover your dog with blankets and let your dog warm up gradually.1 Contact your veterinarian if your dog is shivering or extremely lethargic after being out in the cold.4

Talk to your vet. Ask your veterinarian how much cold your dog can handle, especially if your dog is a puppy, a senior, has a health condition or takes medication. If your dog shows signs of being too cold, call your vet.

How long should winter walks be?

Winter walks should be shorter than usual when it is very cold. Keep walks short and watch for signs of hypothermia.3,4

Some dogs may need a coat. Consider a sweater or dog coat if your dog has a short coat or seems bothered by the cold.4

How do you protect your dog's paws in winter?

To protect your dog's paws in winter, consider booties, check the paws often and clean them after walks. Booties can protect paw pads from the cold and from rock salt; just make sure they fit.1,4

  • Before walks: consider keeping the hair between the paw pads short to help prevent ice balls, and applying paw balm to the pads before each walk.3
  • During the walk: longer-haired dogs may collect ice and snow on their feet, so check them regularly.3
  • After the walk: wipe or wash your dog's feet, legs and belly to remove packed snow, ice, salt and de-icing chemicals.4

Check your dog's paws often for signs of irritation or injury, too.4

What winter hazards should dog owners know about?

Antifreeze, de-icers and frozen water are 3 hazards veterinary sources flag. The active ingredient in most brands of antifreeze is ethylene glycol, which is sweet-tasting and highly toxic, so store it out of reach and clean up any spills right away.3

Salt and most de-icers can also be toxic to dogs, and pet-safe de-icing products are the recommended choice.3,4 Stay away from frozen ponds, lakes and streams as well.4

Frequently asked questions.

What temperature is too cold for a dog to be outside?

There is no single number for every dog. Veterinary schools say to bring pets indoors or provide adequate shelter below freezing (32°F or 0°C), and that puppies and short-haired pets should come inside below 40°F (about 4°C) and outdoor pets below 20°F (about -7°C).1,2

How long can a dog stay out in freezing weather?

Washington State says most dogs, especially those with thinner coats, should not be exposed to freezing temperatures for more than 10 to 15 minutes without access to a warmer space.1

Do dogs need coats in winter?

Some do. Consider a sweater or dog coat if your dog has a short coat or seems bothered by the cold, and keep in mind that small and short-haired dogs may need one even for short walks in cold, windy weather.3,4

What are the signs a dog is too cold?

Signs a dog may be too cold include shivering, anxiety, moving slowly, lifting the paws or seeming reluctant to walk. If your dog is shivering or extremely lethargic after being out in the cold, contact your veterinarian.3,4

How cold is too cold to walk a dog?

There is no set cutoff. Shorten walks in very cold weather and watch for signs of hypothermia.3,4
