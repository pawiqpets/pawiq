How do I calm my dog during fireworks?

A safe room, an early walk, a secure yard and a current ID tag make up this New Year's Eve plan for fireworks.

Written by the pawIQ team · Last reviewed November 12, 2026 · 5 min read

To help your dog stay calm during fireworks, give them a quiet place to hide, close the windows and blinds, and play the radio or TV.1,2 On New Year's Eve (NYE), plan the day too: exercise before the fireworks start, an early walk, and a current ID tag.2,3,4

Why fireworks scare so many dogs

Fear of loud noises is very common in pets, and fireworks are an especially scary sound for most dogs.1

Every year, some dogs get so scared that they may escape their yard or house and risk getting lost or hit by a car.1

Noise phobia can get worse as a pet ages.5 In an owner survey of 13,715 pet dogs from 264 breeds in Finland, 32% of the dogs were highly fearful of at least 1 noise.6 Fear of fireworks was the most common noise fear in that survey, reported for 26% of the dogs.6 If your dog was scared last year, start planning before New Year's Eve.

Sound training starts weeks before New Year's Eve

The University of California, Davis School of Veterinary Medicine recommends starting sound training weeks to months before the holiday.1 The method, called counter conditioning and desensitization, slowly replaces a fearful reaction with a relaxed one.1

Play a recording of fireworks so quietly that you can barely hear it, and give your dog a few small, tasty treats.1 Make sure your dog is not fearful at that level, then turn the volume up a little and give more treats.1 Keep each session very short, hold several a day, and always end with your dog relaxed and readily taking treats.1

Watch for signs of fear, such as ears back, a tucked tail, panting, trembling or drooling, or a dog that stops taking treats.1 If you see them, never punish your dog.1

Setting up a safe space for New Year's Eve

A safe space for New Year's Eve is a small, escape-proof room your dog already knows.2,3 A quiet bedroom, a crate inside the house or a dog bed can all work as a safe place to hide.1

Closing the windows and blinds and playing the radio or TV may help block out some of the sights and sounds.1 The Purdue University College of Veterinary Medicine's tips call for a darkened room.2

Set up the safe room before the festivities begin.2

Planning New Year's Eve around your dog

A good NYE plan puts walks and exercise early in the day, then keeps your dog inside once the fireworks begin.2,3,4

Exercise your dog before the fireworks start, which can help them feel less anxious.2 Walk your dog earlier in the day, when fewer fireworks are going off.4

Never leave your dog alone outdoors, even tied up or in a fenced yard.5 Dogs can escape and get lost, or hurt themselves by chewing or choking on a leash.5

Check that your yard is secure enough to keep your dog in if neighbors set off fireworks at an unexpected time.3 If guests will be coming and going, ask them to help keep an eye on your dog, and put notes on exit doors and gates.3

Check ID tags ahead of time and make sure the information on them is up to date.2,3,5 The American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) suggests talking with your vet about microchipping if your dog is not chipped yet.3 A current photo of your dog helps if they get loose.2,3

New Year's Eve countdown plan at a glance

A New Year's Eve plan for your dog has 6 stages: the weeks before, the days before, NYE morning, before the fireworks start, during the fireworks and New Year's Day morning.

How to comfort your dog during fireworks

To comfort your dog once fireworks start, stay with them and reward calm behavior.1,2

If your dog needs to go out, use a leash.2,5 Some dogs love to chase spinning fireworks on the ground.5

Going to a public show? Leave your dog at home.2,3,5 Also keep sparklers away from your dog, since kids waving them can upset a family pet.5

What to do after the fireworks end

Check your yard for firework debris before your dog explores.3,5 Debris can land in your yard even if you did not set off fireworks yourself, and curious pets may pick it up to play with or eat.3

Talk to your vet. If your dog has reacted strongly to loud noises before, talk to your vet before New Year's Eve.1,3 Your vet can talk through options, such as behavior training to help your dog get used to the sounds.3

Frequently asked questions.

Will my dog get used to fireworks?

A dog can learn to relax around fireworks with gradual sound training, but the change does not happen quickly.1 If the sight of fireworks or the smell of gunpowder also scares your dog, each trigger needs its own training, such as a video recording of fireworks for the sight.1

Can I leave my dog alone during fireworks?

A dog that fears fireworks should be supervised while the fireworks go off, according to the University of California, Davis.1
