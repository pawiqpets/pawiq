Why does my dog get an upset stomach after boarding, travel or a change in routine?

What stress, a diet change and car travel can do to a dog's stomach, and the signs and timing that mean it is time to call your vet.

Written by the pawIQ team · Last reviewed November 15, 2026 · 4 min read

Stress, a sudden change in diet or a car ride can each upset a dog's stomach after boarding or travel.1,2 Many cases of stomach upset resolve on their own.3 Watch for these warning signs.

  • If your dog goes 24 hours without eating or drinking, see a vet right away.4
  • Extreme lethargy also needs a vet right away.4
  • Bloody diarrhea needs a vet right away.4 Blood in the vomit is also a reason to call your vet.3
  • Vomiting or diarrhea that has lasted more than 24 hours should be seen by a vet within the next 24 hours.4

Stress and diet changes can cause diarrhea

Stress can cause diarrhea in dogs, and so can a sudden change in diet.1 Stress from a recent move, getting into the garbage, eating too many table scraps and intestinal worms are all obvious causes.1 When diarrhea from an obvious cause passes quickly, it can be considered normal and self-resolving.1

A new place can also shift how a dog eats and acts. The American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) notes that in unfamiliar surroundings, a pet's appetite, energy and behavior may change.5

Skipped meals and loose stool are common early in boarding

Some adjustment, such as a skipped meal or mild loose stool, is common when a dog is boarded.6 Texas A&M says it is really common for animals not to eat for the first 12 to 24 hours, and mild loose stool, pacing, panting or hiding can also show up early on.6

After pickup, dogs commonly need a short time to readjust: they may want extra attention, sleep more or take a day to settle back into their routine.6 Persistent or severe signs, such as vomiting, diarrhea, coughing, lethargy or big behavior changes, should not be ignored.6

Can a car ride cause a dog to vomit or have diarrhea?

Yes, a car ride can make a dog vomit or have diarrhea when the dog gets motion sickness.2 The Merck Veterinary Manual lists nausea, excessive drooling and vomiting as signs, and says a dog may also have diarrhea.2 Occasionally a dog also has a poor appetite for several hours afterward.2 Motion sickness is usually seen during travel by land, sea or air, and signs usually disappear when the vehicle stops moving.2

Fear of the vehicle may play a part, and signs may show up even when the vehicle is not moving.2 In some cases, conditioning a dog to travel can overcome motion sickness.2 The AVMA suggests giving your dog chances to get used to car travel before any long car trip.5

Steady meals, bathroom breaks and water while you travel

A good place to start is keeping your dog's regular feeding and bathroom-break routines steady while you travel with your dog.5 Add 3 more steps:

  • Do a daily health check on your dog while you are away from home, noting appetite, energy and behavior.5
  • If your dog has diarrhea, make sure plenty of water is available, but never force your dog to drink.1
  • Skip table scraps and high-fat foods while your dog's stomach settles.3

When to call the vet about an upset stomach

Signs of an upset stomach in a dog call for a vet right away or within 24 hours, and the table below sorts them by urgency.4

When to see the vet, by urgency
What you seeWhen to see the vet
Bloody, foul-smelling or uncontrollable diarrhea, or thick black stoolsRight away.4
Not eating or drinking for 24 hoursRight away.4
Extreme lethargyRight away.4
An unvaccinated puppy with vomiting or diarrheaAlways, because parvovirus can cause extreme dehydration and death within 24 hours without supportive care.3
A puppy with more than 1 or 2 episodes of vomiting or diarrheaNeeds veterinary care, because puppies become dehydrated very quickly.3
Vomiting or diarrhea that has lasted more than 24 hoursWithin the next 24 hours.4
Long-term vomiting, vomiting more than 1 or 2 times a day, or vomiting with blood, abdominal pain, weakness, fever or dehydrationNeeds a more detailed veterinary examination.7

Packing for your next trip with your dog

Pack enough food to last the entire trip, plus water in case none is available at a stop.5

Talk to your vet. Your vet knows your dog's history and can tell you what is normal for your dog after a trip. Note when the signs started, what your dog ate and any change in routine, then bring those notes to the visit.

Frequently asked questions.

What can I feed a dog with an upset stomach?

A dog with an upset stomach that still wants to eat and drink can have a bland, low-fat diet in the short term, such as boiled skinless chicken or turkey breast mixed with white rice.3 A bland diet like this is not balanced, so it should not be fed for more than a couple of days.1

What should I do if my dog is vomiting after boarding?

A dog that is vomiting after boarding should get food and water the way your vet directs.7 Vomiting and diarrhea can cause life-threatening dehydration if medical care is not provided.3
