Why do dogs eat grass?

Many dogs eat grass, and it is usually normal. Here is what veterinary research says about why, when to call your vet, and how to keep lawn snacking safe.

Written by the pawIQ team · Last reviewed November 6, 2026 · 5 min read

Dogs eat grass for reasons researchers have not fully pinned down, but it is very common and generally considered normal dog behavior. In a large owner survey, only 9% of plant-eating dogs were reported to often seem sick before eating plants, and 22% to often vomit afterward, so watch for a sudden change in the habit and what is on the grass.1,2,4

How common is grass eating in dogs?

Grass eating is very common in dogs. In a study from a veterinary teaching hospital, published in Applied Animal Behaviour Science, 79% of 47 owners whose dogs had daily access to plants said their dog had eaten grass or other plants.1

The researchers then ran an internet survey of owners of plant-eating dogs and analyzed 1,571 responses. Overall, 68% of those dogs ate plants daily or weekly, and grass was the plant owners saw their dog eat most often.1

Is it normal for dogs to eat grass?

Yes, eating grass appears to be normal for most dogs. The study's findings support the view that plant eating is a normal behavior of domestic dogs.1

Sex, reproductive status, breed group and diet type showed no clear link to how often dogs ate plants. Younger dogs ate plants more often than older dogs.1

The University of Wisconsin-Madison School of Veterinary Medicine agrees: if your dog commonly eats grass, it is not an immediate cause for alarm.2

Do dogs eat grass to make themselves throw up?

Most dogs probably do not eat grass to make themselves throw up. In the survey, only 9% of dogs were reported to frequently seem ill before eating plants, and only 22% to frequently vomit afterward.1

Nausea may be a reason some dogs eat grass, but not always.2 Vomiting itself has many possible causes, including irritating substances and poisons, so ask your vet if it keeps happening.3

What are the main reasons dogs eat grass?

The exact reason dogs eat grass is not clearly known.2 Researchers and veterinarians have suggested 3 possible explanations:

  • Normal scavenging: dogs are scavengers, and trying unusual things is common for them.2
  • A possible inherited habit: the survey authors suggested plant eating may reflect a predisposition passed down from wild canid ancestors, possibly as a way to expel intestinal parasites. They noted that controlled studies could test this idea.1
  • An upset stomach, sometimes: nausea may play a part for some dogs, though few grass eaters are reported to frequently seem ill first.1,2

When should you call your vet about grass eating?

Talk to your vet if grass eating is new for your dog or comes with other changes. A new behavior may indicate a change in your dog's health. Look for other changes in behavior or daily habits, including changes in diet.2

Vomiting is the other thing to watch. The Merck Veterinary Manual says vomiting that happens more often than once or twice a day, or that comes with any of these signs, needs a closer veterinary exam:3

  • Blood in the vomit
  • Belly (abdominal) pain
  • Weakness or depression (low energy, withdrawn)
  • Fever
  • Dehydration or weight loss

Long-term, repeated vomiting may be associated with weakness, lethargy, weight loss and dehydration, so a regular pattern is worth a vet visit.3

Are lawn chemicals dangerous if my dog eats grass?

Some lawn chemicals are dangerous to dogs, so it pays to know what has been applied. Fertilizers are spread in dilute amounts but can cause vomiting and bloating if a dog eats a large quantity. Most herbicides and fungicides are heavily diluted too, and the most likely problem if a dog eats them is vomiting, unless eaten in concentrated form.4

Pesticides are the bigger concern. They are generally toxic to pets, and insecticides affect the nervous system of dogs and need immediate veterinary care.4

A few habits lower the risk:

  • When in doubt, keep your dog off treated areas until the lawn has been thoroughly watered and allowed to fully dry.5
  • If you use a lawn care service, tell them you have pets.5
  • Use pet-safe lawn and garden products when possible.5
  • Store all lawn and garden products out of reach or locked away.4

Which plants should dogs not eat?

Some plants and fungi around the home can be toxic. Texas A&M's College of Veterinary Medicine lists sago palms and rose laurel among non-garden toxic plants to watch for, and advises keeping pets away from mushrooms and compost piles.5

Other good habits: use a phone app to identify the plants in your yard, supervise pets outside, and do not let them drink from ponds or standing water, where blue-green algae can grow.5

What should you do if your dog eats something toxic?

If your dog eats something toxic, act right away. Remove your pet from the source, then call your veterinarian, an emergency veterinary hospital or a pet poison helpline immediately. Do not try to treat your dog at home unless a veterinarian tells you to.5

Bring the plant sample or product label if you can.5 If it was a lawn or garden product, take a photo of the packaging so you can tell a poison center the active ingredient.4

Talk to your vet. If your dog suddenly starts eating grass, or vomits often, check in with your veterinarian. They can look at the whole picture, including diet and daily routine.

Frequently asked questions.

Is it bad if my dog eats grass every day?

Not necessarily. In a large owner survey, 68% of plant-eating dogs ate plants daily or weekly, and the researchers said the findings support plant eating being normal dog behavior.1 If daily grass eating is new for your dog, or comes with vomiting or other changes, call your vet.2,3

Should I stop my dog from eating grass?

If your dog commonly eats grass, it is not an immediate cause for alarm, according to UW-Madison's veterinary school.2 When in doubt, keep your dog off treated lawns until they have been thoroughly watered and allowed to fully dry, and away from toxic plants and mushrooms.5

Why does my dog eat grass and then throw up?

Nausea may be part of it for some dogs, but in a large owner survey, 78% of owners said their dog rarely vomited after eating plants. Call your vet if vomiting happens more than once or twice a day, contains blood, or comes with weakness, belly pain or fever.1,3

Do younger dogs eat grass and other plants more often?

Yes, based on owner reports. In the survey, younger dogs ate plants more often than older dogs and were less likely to seem ill beforehand or vomit afterward.1
